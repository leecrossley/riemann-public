\caption{Step~(2) of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:windowed-landau}, with
illustrative values $\sigma=40$ and $\delta=1$. The curves are the
kernels $u^ke^{-\sigma u}$ of the Taylor coefficients $c_k(\sigma)$,
scaled to total mass~$1$; the kernel for $k$ peaks at $u=k/\sigma$. In
the variable $u=\log y$ a window $[Y,Y^{1+\delta}]$ is the interval
$[U,(1+\delta)U]$, $U=\log Y$; here $U=1.5$ and $U'=4$. The kernel for
$k=90$ has a proportion $2.7\cdot10^{-3}$ of its mass outside $[U,2U]$,
and the kernel for $k=240$ a proportion $1.7\cdot10^{-6}$ outside
$[U',2U']$, so each of these coefficients sees essentially one window.
The kernel for $k=140$ has about $91\%$ of its mass outside both
windows; the argument needs no bound on such coefficients, because the
lower bound of step~(4) holds for every~$k$. The proportions are computed
for these illustrative values only.}
